## What "Unmetered" Actually Means (And Why It Matters More Than "Unlimited")

Here's the thing most comparison articles won't tell you: "unmetered" and "unlimited" are not the same animal. Unlimited bandwidth, in marketing-speak, almost always has a fair-use clause buried somewhere in paragraph 47 of the terms of service. Unmetered is different — it means the provider isn't counting your bytes against a monthly quota. What you're really buying is a port speed (1Gbps, 10Gbps, sometimes higher), and as long as you stay within that port's capacity, you can push as much data as the pipe will physically carry.

This matters most for a few specific workloads:

- **Streaming and media delivery**, where sustained high throughput is the actual product
- **Game servers**, where latency and uptime are non-negotiable and traffic spikes follow patch days and tournaments
- **Large-scale backups and replication**, where monthly transfer would blow past any metered cap in a week
- **High-traffic web apps** that get featured somewhere and suddenly triple their visitors overnight

For all of these, the predictability of unmetered pricing is the entire point. You're not optimizing for the cheapest possible month — you're optimizing for the worst-case month not destroying your budget.
